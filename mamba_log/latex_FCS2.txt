\begin{abstract}
Strong navigation after imitation learning does not establish generalization to
larger crowds, nor does adding a recursive belief identify the source of failure.
We examine an imitation-initialized continuous policy with a masked set encoder
and Bayesian motion-mode features, subsequently optimized by proximal policy
optimization (PPO). A frozen stage comparison evaluates the actual unicycle
planning teacher, the pre-PPO DAgger student, and nine PPO checkpoints spanning
three belief inputs and three training seeds. The experiment comprises 4,400
episodes over 200 physical layouts, four crowd configurations, and two behavior
profiles. In the 20-person square configuration, the teacher achieves 90\%
nominal and 80\% held-out success, whereas the pre-PPO student achieves 4\%
and 6\%. Teacher--student differences are 86 percentage points
(paired 95\% interval: 76--94) and 74 points (62--86).
PPO retains the five-person nominal performance but does not recover the
large-crowd deficit; complete mode posteriors do not consistently improve on
point estimates or omitted belief features. Thus, in this implementation, the
large deployment deficit is already present before reinforcement learning.
The findings establish an achievable planning reference and localize a training
and generalization problem. They do not establish the sufficiency of a new risk
representation or an independent Bayesian contribution.
\end{abstract}
\keywords{Crowd navigation; Imitation learning; Bayesian belief; Proximal policy optimization; Generalization; Stage-wise evaluation}

\section{INTRODUCTION}
\label{sec:intro}
Learning continuous crowd navigation from a planning teacher is attractive:
imitation supplies basic control competence, and reinforcement learning can
subsequently optimize closed-loop behavior. Recursive Bayesian features can
summarize observations of pedestrian motion without a temporal neural policy.
However, several distinct claims are easily conflated: that the interface accepts
variable-size crowds, that the learned policy generalizes to them, and that a
particular belief representation contributes useful decision information.

These claims require different evidence. A permutation-invariant encoder removes
dependence on input order, not dependence on crowd cardinality or geometry.
Similarly, a successful teacher demonstrates an achievable policy, not an
optimal upper bound. Poor student performance could precede reinforcement
learning, be induced by it, or reflect limitations of the teacher itself.
Comparing only final policies cannot distinguish these explanations.

We therefore retain an existing imitation--belief--PPO architecture and evaluate
its successive stages under identical deployment conditions. The study asks:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Can the actual unicycle teacher navigate the configurations in which the
learned policies fail?
\item Is that deficit already present in the DAgger student before PPO?
\item Does the trained full-posterior policy reduce the deficit relative to
MAP or No-Belief policies with the same initialization and training budget?
\end{enumerate}
The first two questions prevent a misleading search for a Bayesian repair to a
failure that has not been localized. The third tests the current belief input,
not the universal necessity of Bayesian inference.

Our contribution at this stage is a controlled diagnosis of one learning
pipeline, rather than a new algorithm. We verify checkpoint provenance and
action conversion, compare teacher and student using the same nonholonomic
executor, and separate changes in population, layout family, and behavior.
The results identify a substantial, achievable teacher--student gap in square
crowds that exists before PPO. A method for closing that gap remains to be
developed and tested; we do not rename the diagnosis as a validated method.

\section{RELATED WORK}
\label{sec:related}
DAgger addresses the discrepancy between expert-visited and learner-visited
states by collecting expert labels along learner trajectories
\cite{ross2011dagger}. This motivates the original student's training, but does
not guarantee transfer to crowd configurations excluded from data collection.
PPO updates a stochastic policy using on-policy samples and a clipped surrogate
objective \cite{schulman2017ppo}. We use its standard state-value formulation
through Stable-Baselines3 \cite{raffin2021sb3}, not an action-value or safety-cost
critic.

Deep Sets and attention-based set models provide architectures for unordered
inputs \cite{zaheer2017deepsets,lee2019settransformer}. Attention has also been
used directly in crowd-navigation policies \cite{chen2019crowd}.
Our masked attention-plus-max encoder is an implementation choice, not a new
set-learning principle. Its support for different numbers of tokens must be
distinguished from statistical generalization beyond its training distribution.
Work on reinforcement-learning generalization makes the importance of distinct
training and deployment environments explicit \cite{cobbe2019generalization}.

Belief states provide a principled representation of history in partially
observable decision problems when the model and posterior are appropriate
\cite{kaelbling1998pomdp}. An approximate motion-mode posterior is not
automatically a sufficient statistic for a different simulator. In particular,
the mode labels used here are fitted dynamical hypotheses, not known human
intentions. Our pedestrian simulator derives nominal motion from ORCA
\cite{vandenberg2011orca}; robots and pedestrians do not share its reciprocity
assumption because the robot is invisible to the pedestrian policies.

Finally, finite-seed evaluation can produce unstable performance comparisons
\cite{agarwal2021precipice}. We report the individual PPO seeds and paired
layout intervals. Three PPO seeds do not constitute three independent
imitation-learning pipelines: all inherit the same DAgger source.

\section{THE FROZEN LEARNING PIPELINE}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Observations, beliefs, and actions}
The robot receives the current position, velocity, and radius of every
pedestrian. There is no sensor occlusion in this branch. Uncertainty concerns
the fitted motion model and unobserved behavior evolution, not hidden pedestrian
existence. For pedestrian $i$, the recursive model maintains
\begin{equation}
 b_t^i(k)=\Prob_M(z_t^i=k\mid o_{0:t}),\qquad k=1,2,3,
\end{equation}
where $M$ denotes the fitted model. Its Gaussian dynamic hypotheses use
linear transitions $x_{t+1}=A_kx_t+\epsilon_k$ and a mode-transition matrix.
The implementation uses 50 particles per active tracker and an episode-seeded
random stream. The robot-action coefficients are zero: the simulator's
pedestrians do not respond to the invisible robot. This physical constraint is
preserved across all arms.

Each human token has nine fields: ego-relative position (two), relative velocity
(two), radius, and four belief fields. Fixed physical scales are 10 m for
position, 2 m/s for velocity, and 0.3 m for radius. The four belief fields are
\begin{equation}
 \beta_t^i =
 \begin{cases}
  (0,0,0,0), & \text{No-Belief},\\
  (e_{\arg\max_k b_t^i(k)},0), & \text{MAP},\\
  (b_t^i(1),b_t^i(2),b_t^i(3),H(b_t^i)/\log 3), & \text{FULL}.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here FULL means the complete \emph{three-mode marginal}, not a complete joint
posterior over all human trajectories. Invalid padded slots have an explicit
mask. The storage maximum is 20; no test entity is silently truncated.

The robot token contains seven normalized physical fields: relative goal,
current velocity, radius, preferred speed, and remaining time. Two previous
executed-action fields and a route-history scalar give ten inputs:
\begin{equation}
 r_{t+1}=0.7r_t+0.3\,\omega_t/1.2,\qquad r_0=0.
\end{equation}
Thus No-Belief is not strictly memoryless: all arms receive this same limited
execution history.

Actions are continuous, $v\in[0,1]$ m/s and
$\omega\in[-1.2,1.2]$ rad/s. With $\Delta t=0.25$ s, the simulator executes
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \theta_{t+1}&=\theta_t+\omega_t\Delta t,\\
 p_{t+1}&=p_t+v_t\Delta t
       [\cos\theta_{t+1},\sin\theta_{t+1}]^\top .
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The episode ends by 140 steps, or 35 s. Collision auditing includes overlap
along the actual executed robot--pedestrian segments, not only the simulator's
native collision flag.

\subsection{Set encoding and imitation initialization}
Shared human layers map each token through widths 64 and 64. A 32-dimensional
robot embedding conditions masked attention; its weighted human embedding and
a masked maximum are concatenated with the robot embedding. A 160-to-192
fusion layer supplies the actor. There is no explicit count feature. The policy
MLP has widths $[256,256]$.

The source policy was trained by behavioral cloning followed by student-executed
DAgger in five-person nominal circle scenarios. During DAgger, the student
executes its action and the teacher supplies a separate label; failed student
episodes remain labelable, and original demonstrations remain in the data pool.
The final round-16 checkpoint is fixed for this study. Historical extensions
were adaptive engineering development, not a pre-registered optimal-budget
comparison.

The teacher is the actual continuous unicycle CEM controller, using a 16-step
horizon, population 512, four iterations, and a 0.50 m human margin.
It predicts humans with constant velocity and retains its previous plan as a
warm start. It does \emph{not} use the GDBN posterior in this pipeline.
An archived experiment used a different GDBN teacher adapter; its qualification
receipt is not evidence about the present source policy.

\subsection{PPO optimization}
The DAgger actor is converted exactly into the mean of a physical-space Gaussian
policy. Its bounded mean is $c+d\odot\tanh(f_\theta)$, with
$c=(0.5,0)$ and $d=(0.5,1.2)$. Training samples an ordinary Gaussian and clips
actions to physical limits; this is not a tanh-squashed distribution.
Evaluation uses the deterministic bounded mean.

Initial belief-input weights are zero in both policy and value encoders, but
remain trainable. All three arms therefore start with the same physical policy,
rather than one arm receiving a stronger navigation initialization.
The value network has an independent encoder and widths $[96,96]$.
Its encoder is initialized from the same source; its value head is newly
initialized. No old optimizer or action-value critic is transferred.

The policy objective is the usual PPO surrogate,
\begin{equation}
 L^{\mathrm{clip}}(\theta)=\E_t\!\left[
 \min\{\rho_t(\theta)\hat A_t,
 \operatorname{clip}(\rho_t(\theta),0.9,1.1)\hat A_t\}\right].
\end{equation}
There is no concurrent BC loss, cost critic, safety replay, residual action
network, or action grid. The frozen qualification configuration uses learning
rate $10^{-5}$, 2,048-step rollouts, batch size 256, three epochs, discount 0.99,
GAE parameter 0.95, and zero entropy coefficient. The KL early-stop threshold
is disabled for all arms; the ten rollouts generate exactly 240 Adam steps per
checkpoint. Each seed receives 20,480 environment steps on
\texttt{train\_nonstationary}, with five humans only.
This is a finite completed budget, not evidence of asymptotic convergence.

\section{EVALUATION PROTOCOL}
\label{sec:protocol}
\subsection{Frozen configurations and pairing}
We evaluate one teacher, one pre-PPO source, and nine final PPO checkpoints
(three arms at seeds 2407, 4807, and 7207).
There are 50 layouts for each configuration in Table~\ref{tab:scenes}.
Each layout is evaluated under nominal and held-out behavior, resulting in
$4\times 50\times 2\times(2+3\times3)=4,400$ episodes.
The 150 episodes reported for a PPO arm within a cell reuse 50 layouts across
three policies; they are not 150 independent layouts.

Nominal behavior has no scheduled intervention. PPO training used event
probability 0.025 per eligible pedestrian-step, duration 2--6 steps, turn angles
20--50 degrees, and slow scales 0.35--0.70. Held-out behavior uses probability
0.035, duration 4--9 steps, turn angles 55--85 degrees, and slow scales
0.15--0.45. The interventions are stop, slow, left turn, and right turn.
Their labels and future random draws are unavailable to both teacher and policy.
The three GDBN modes are not identified with these four intervention categories.

Layouts use seeds $170000000+10000j+i$, where $j$ indexes the four configurations
and $i=0,\ldots,49$. Case IDs are $510000+1000j+i$.
Nominal and held-out profiles share initial geometry, and all policies within
each cell share both geometry and behavior seeds.
Human goals, starts, radii, and preferred speeds, together with the initial robot
state, are hashed to verify layout matching.

The historical batch spawn routine could permit human--human initial overlap
because previously sampled humans were not inserted until after batch
generation. For deployment auditing only, an instance-local wrapper inserts
each sampled human immediately. All policies receive this identical corrected
generator. The minimum initial pairwise surface clearance over the audit is
0.201742 m; no action-bound violation occurs.
Historical checkpoints were not retrained after this generator correction,
which limits causal attribution to crowd size alone.

\subsection{Provenance and statistical scope}
Before evaluation, three archived IL episodes and three archived PPO episodes
were replayed through the original generator: outcome, length, and return
matched exactly. On the IL traces, the action difference between the stored
source and its PPO-mean conversion was zero. Abstract-syntax comparisons
confirmed that the CV teacher's core classes match the qualified historical
version. Production encoder and history-wrapper definitions are used directly
for inference without instantiating obsolete training classes.

Success, collision, and timeout are mutually exclusive.
Reported return is the undiscounted sum of actual environment rewards.
The task reward is unchanged, including success $+1$, collision $-0.5$,
timeout $-0.5$, progress coefficient 0.01, and per-step penalty $-0.003$.
We do not compare parallel wall-clock evaluation times as planner latency.

For each pair of methods, 5,000 bootstrap draws resample layout indices
jointly. PPO comparisons also resample the three policy-seed indices.
The single teacher and single IL source are not replicated as independent
training seeds. Intervals are pointwise exploratory 95\% intervals, not
family-wise corrected tests or equivalence bounds. The configurations, final
checkpoints, sample counts, and contrasts were recorded before these outcomes.
Earlier deployment failures motivated the audit, so this is a new paired
diagnosis, not an independently discovered research hypothesis.

\section{RESULTS}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{The deployment deficit precedes PPO}
Table~\ref{tab:success} reports all eight cells.
The nominal five-person circle remains navigable by both the teacher and
the learned policies. At 20 humans in the square configuration, the teacher
succeeds on 45/50 layouts whereas the pre-PPO actor succeeds on 2/50.
The paired success difference is 86 percentage points, with interval [76,94].
Under held-out behavior, the corresponding counts are 40/50 and 3/50,
a difference of 74 points [62,86].

The layout pairing is informative: on S20 nominal, 43 layouts succeed only
with the teacher and none succeed only with IL. On S20 held-out, the counts
are 37 and zero. These are achievable trajectory differences under the same
executor, not a comparison against a holonomic controller.
They rule out PPO as a necessary cause of this deployment failure because
the source actor already fails before any PPO update.

The deficit is not confined to population increase. With the same count of ten,
the pre-PPO actor achieves 84\% nominal success in circles and 42\% in squares.
Those distributions differ in geometry and scale, so this is evidence of a
broader configuration shift, not a density-only causal experiment.
Behavior shift further reduces C10 IL success from 84\% to 44\%.

\subsection{Failure type and incremental PPO effects}
S20 IL failures are collisions, not merely slow goal approach:
48/50 nominal and 47/50 held-out episodes collide. Median collision time
among these failures is 3.25 s in both profiles.
Only two collisions in each cell occur within the first second, and initial
clearance checks pass. This does not establish a representation bottleneck,
but it excludes treating all failures as invalid overlapping spawns or timeout.

Table~\ref{tab:densecounts} supplies seed-level outcomes for the hardest cell.
The modest changes after PPO do not recover the teacher's performance.
For example, S20 nominal FULL improves over IL by 1.33 points
[-3.33,7.33]; held-out FULL improves by 1.33 points [-5.33,9.33].
Five-person nominal performance is retained, while held-out C5 changes are
small and uncertain. Hence neither \emph{PPO destroyed a good dense-crowd
policy} nor \emph{PPO repaired its generalization} describes these checkpoints.

\subsection{No consistent advantage of the current belief}
FULL minus No-Belief nominal success differences range from $-4.00$ points
in S10 to $+1.33$ in C10. On S20 they are $-1.33$ [-5.33,0.00]
nominal and $+1.33$ [-4.00,8.68] held-out.
FULL minus MAP in S20 is $+2.67$ points in each profile, but intervals are
[-1.33,8.00] and [-6.00,12.00], respectively.
These results do not establish equivalence, and do not support a robust
Bayesian improvement.

Return is consistent with the large stage gap: S20 nominal mean return is
0.7023 for the teacher and $-0.5006$ for IL; held-out means are
0.5497 and $-0.4645$. The paired differences are 1.2028 [1.0470,1.3364]
and 1.0143 [0.8310,1.1840]. FULL reaches $-0.4857$ and $-0.4460$.
Thus the teacher--student difference is not solely an artifact of reporting
success rather than reward.

\section{INTERPRETATION AND NEXT METHOD GATE}
\label{sec:discussion}
The actionable finding is an out-of-distribution teacher--student gap, already
present in the imitation policy and not repaired by the tested PPO budget.
The study does not isolate its cause among training coverage, learned
aggregation, policy capacity, route-memory mismatch, or optimization.
It therefore supports investigating generalization of the learned controller,
not immediately replacing the Bayesian estimator.

Three interpretations must be rejected. First, the actual teacher uses CV,
so these results do not establish that Bayesian information was already
distilled away into every student. Even a belief-aware teacher would not make
unavailable inference-time information automatically recoverable by its student.
Second, a successful teacher is an achievable reference, not a task upper
bound; its failure would not prove impossibility.
Third, a fixed-size risk map is not automatically a sufficient statistic,
and permutation invariance is not invariance to adding pedestrians.

For a subsequent method study, the cheapest strong control is equal-budget
training-coverage expansion using the unchanged SetEncoder, before attributing
the deficit to its representation. That experiment has \emph{not} been run here.
It requires a separately declared mixed-population training protocol and
fresh held-out layouts. It must distinguish zero-shot five-person-only training
from training that already includes ten- and twenty-person crowds; the latter
is a coverage reference, not an identically informed zero-shot competitor.
Matched teacher queries, simulator transitions, policy capacity, and optimization
steps should be reported separately, rather than declaring equal compute from
episode count alone.

A new belief-derived representation would then require a mechanism and
comparisons against the unchanged encoder, matched simple uncertainty
representations, and appropriate short-history controls. Matching a stronger
training baseline could still be useful if demonstrated with less data or
compute; conversely, a larger success number alone does not identify a
Bayesian contribution. These are prospective requirements, not conclusions
of this audit. We retain IL initialization, recursive belief, and PPO as the
target architecture without claiming that their current combination is novel.

\subsection{Limitations}
There is one IL training lineage and only three PPO seeds, with 50 paired
layouts per configuration. The corrected deployment generator differs from
the historical training generator. Circle--square comparisons also change
layout scale and goal distributions. The held-out profile was defined in
earlier work; only the present layout instances are newly assigned.
There is no occlusion, no sensor-noise deployment test, and no robot hardware
experiment. We do not benchmark sample efficiency, claim that PPO converged,
or infer impossibility from a negative finite-budget comparison.
Counterfactual representation and mixed-training experiments remain necessary
before presenting a new generalization method.

\section{CONCLUSION}
The teacher--IL--PPO comparison localizes the major large-crowd deficit before
reinforcement learning. In a shared unicycle environment, the CV teacher
reaches 90\% nominal and 80\% held-out success with twenty pedestrians, whereas
the fixed DAgger student reaches 4\% and 6\%. The tested PPO policies do not
close this gap, regardless of whether they receive no mode belief, a MAP
estimate, or the full mode marginal. This is a concrete target for a method
study, not evidence that Bayesian inference is necessary or that a proposed
replacement representation already works.
